# THINGS REASONING WOULD GET WRONG
#
#   * **`avg(interval)` is `sum / count` through the same code `interval / n` is.** 180 measured
#     pairs — twenty values by nine divisors — agree byte for byte, which is also why the
#     accumulator must keep the exact sum and divide once: the mean of two means is not the mean,
#     and here it is not even the same rounding.
#   * **`1 mon / 3` is `10 days`, and only rounding makes it so.** `(1/3) * 30` is
#     `9.999999999999998` in a double, so scaling each field and truncating answers
#     `9 days 23:59:59.999999` — wrong in the last microsecond and unrecognisable in print.
#   * **`1 mon / 9` is `3 days 07:59:59.9712`, not `3 days 08:00:00`.** The month's remainder is
#     rounded to six decimals **as a number of days** (`0.333333`) before its whole days are split
#     off; rounding the seconds instead gives the wrong answer here.
#   * **`100 days / 7` keeps full precision** (`14 days 06:51:25.714286`): the flattening is
#     confined to the month remainder and does not touch the day remainder.
#   * **The time field is not normalised into days.** `'1 mon 1 day 23:59:59.999999' / 16` is
#     `1 day 24:00:00` — twenty-four hours in a field beside a day field of one.
#   * **The last microsecond rounds ties to even**: `3 us / 2` is `2 us` and `5 us / 2` is `2 us`,
#     which neither truncation nor half-away-from-zero produces.
#   * **`sum(time)` and `avg(time)` exist and are `interval`s**, where `min(time)` is a `time`.
#     `sum(timestamp)`, `avg(timestamp)` and `sum(date)` do not exist at all.
#   * **`sum` overflows**: `22008 interval out of range`, where `/ 0` is `22012 division by zero`.
#   * `DISTINCT` **converts** before it deduplicates: `1 mon` and `30 days` are one value.
